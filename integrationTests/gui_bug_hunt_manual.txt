Steps that check behaviours of the Fearless GUI that no Fearless test can observe.
Each section is one behaviour: the steps, and what a person sees when the GUI follows the design.
"Run P" means: run the portable Fearless (built by Coordinator/Build/src/scripts/DeployPortableFearless.java) with the project folder P as its argument.
The console of that run shows the output and the warnings of the program.

1. A still window allocates nothing per frame for its widgets and its text

Design: StandardLibraryGuiBugs.txt, "A still GUI allocates nothing per frame."
Steps:
1. Run integrationTests/testGuiStill and do not touch the window.
2. In another console, run "jps -l" of the JDK and find the process id of the line ending with "tools.PathClassLoader".
3. Wait one minute, then take two class histograms 10 seconds apart: "jcmd <that process id> GC.class_histogram -all".
Expected: the difference of the two histograms holds about 90 objects per frame (the window renders 30 frames per second), and none of them depends on the widgets or on the text of the window.
They are the event of the frame timer and the copy of the frame onto the window (classes of java.awt, java.util.concurrent, javax.swing, sun.awt and sun.java2d),
the elapsed time of the frame (one _base.Instant$5c$0$1 and one _base.Nat$c$0Instance)
and the scale to device pixels (one io.github.humbleui.skija.Matrix33 and one float[]).
The difference holds no lambda of _base.Sk, no io.github.humbleui.types.Rect and no io.github.humbleui.types.RRect.

2. Typing in a long line takes time proportional to the length of the line

Design: a key typed in a text widget shows on one of the next frames.
Steps:
1. Run integrationTests/testGuiLongLine.
   The area holds one line of 81920 characters, "word " repeated, and does not wrap.
2. Click in the area, press Home, and type x four times.
Expected: each x shows within about two frames (70 ms) of its key, as in a text area holding "word word word word".

3. A still window with a long line draws only the glyphs it shows

Design: drawing a TextArea that shows 100 characters of a long line costs as much as drawing a TextArea whose whole line is those 100 characters.
Steps:
1. Run integrationTests/testGuiLongLine and do not touch the window.
2. Look at the console of the run.
Expected: no warning: the window shows about 100 characters of the line, at 30 frames per second.
A line "Warning [t=...ms]: N repaint frame(s) lost" means the window renders fewer frames per second.

4. A window dragged by its title bar moves back until a third of the screen shows it

Design: StandardLibraryGuiBugs.txt, "The window raises no geometry error: it moves so that min(screen/3, window size) stays visible on each axis, also after the user drags it."
and "The window stays on the primary screen: every move, also a drag by the user, is clamped to the default screen device."
Steps:
1. Run integrationTests/testGuiStill.
   The window opens with the decorations of the desk.
2. Drag the title bar of the window straight down, until only the title bar is above the bottom edge of the work area, and let go.
   Do not touch an edge of the screen with the pointer: on some desks that snaps the window.
3. Drag the title bar of the window to the right, until most of the window is past the right edge of the screen, and let go.
4. On a desk with two monitors, drag the title bar of the window onto the second monitor, and let go.
Expected: at step 2 the window moves up, as soon as the pointer lets go, until a third of the screen height is visible above the bottom edge of the work area.
At step 3 the window moves left until a third of the screen width is visible.
At step 4 the window moves back onto the primary screen, until a third of the screen width is visible.
On the tiling desks (arch_sway, omarchy_hyprland, void_i3) the desk decides where the window is, and the window is not moved.
On fedora_cosmic the window stays where it was let go: the position that Java reads for a window on that desk is not the position on the screen.

5. A still window allocates nothing per frame for a Picture, a Split, a Border, a Sections, a scrolling Pane, a TextField and a TextArea

Design: StandardLibraryGuiBugs.txt, "A still GUI allocates nothing per frame."
Steps:
1. Run integrationTests/testGuiStillWidgets and do not touch the window.
   The window holds a Border: in its north a Split of two Pictures, in its center an expanded Sections whose content is a scrolling Pane, in its south a TextField and a TextArea.
2. Take the two class histograms of section 1, steps 2 and 3.
Expected: the difference of the two histograms holds the objects per frame that section 1 lists for integrationTests/testGuiStill, and as many java.awt.Rectangle per frame.
The difference holds no io.github.humbleui.skija.Paint, no io.github.humbleui.skija.Shader, no io.github.humbleui.types.Rect, no io.github.humbleui.types.RRect and no java.util.ArrayList$Itr.

6. A still window with a clipped widget builds no text per frame

Design: StandardLibraryGuiBugs.txt, "A still GUI allocates nothing per frame."
Steps:
1. Run integrationTests/testGuiStillClipped and do not touch the window.
   The window holds a Label narrower than its text, and a Pane narrower than the Pane inside it.
2. Look at the console of the run.
3. Take the two class histograms of section 1, steps 2 and 3.
Expected: at step 2 the console shows two warnings, one for the text of the Label and one for the Pane inside the Pane, each once.
At step 3 the difference of the two histograms holds the objects per frame that section 1 lists for integrationTests/testGuiStill: no java.lang.String and no byte[].

7. A still window in full screen allocates nothing per frame for its corner

Design: StandardLibraryGuiBugs.txt, "A still GUI allocates nothing per frame."

   The window fills the screen and shows the Fearless corner at its top left.

The difference holds no io.github.humbleui.skija.PathBuilder, no io.github.humbleui.skija.Path, no io.github.humbleui.skija.Paint, no io.github.humbleui.skija.Shader and no io.github.humbleui.types.Rect.

8. A Picture wider than its image shows its background beside the image

Design: a Picture with .keepAspect(True), the default, draws its image as large as its content box allows with the proportions of the image, centered in the content box.
The part of the content box outside the image shows the background of the Picture.
Steps:
1. Run integrationTests/testGuiPictureFill.
   The center of the Border of the window is a Picture with .fill, wider than tall, with a ghost white background.
   The image of the Picture is a blue square whose left edge is a red stripe, 4 of its 40 pixels wide.
Expected: the middle of the window shows a blue square with a thin red stripe along its left side.
Left and right of the square the Picture is ghost white, as the window around the Picture.
